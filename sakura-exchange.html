Program at a Glance

More Than a Visit — A Research-Oriented Exchange

The Sakura Science Exchange Program became one of the most meaningful international academic experiences of my undergraduate journey. It gave me direct exposure to a different research culture while connecting my interests in AI, communication engineering, and agriculture with laboratories, researchers, and real technical systems.

Japan–Bangladesh Friendship through Science

Across seven days, the experience progressed from foundational AI learning to laboratory exploration, research idea generation, presentation, and cultural immersion.

The most important outcome for me was a broader view of research: not just building a model, but understanding the real problem, the surrounding system, experimental constraints, communication, and collaboration.

Day 1

Journey to Japan and a Warm Academic Welcome

My Sakura journey began with the flight to Japan. The first day was about arrival, orientation, and meeting the professors and organizers who would guide the academic experience ahead.

Beginning the Journey

Traveling to Japan for an international science exchange combined excitement with curiosity about a new research environment and academic culture.

Received by the Professors

After arrival, I was welcomed by the University of Yamanashi professors and program hosts. The warm reception immediately made the exchange feel collaborative and supportive.

What I Learned

Academic collaboration begins before the lecture starts. A thoughtful and welcoming environment helps create trust, openness, and confidence for meaningful exchange.

Day 2

Building the Foundations of Agricultural AI

Day 2 focused on foundational learning through sessions on agricultural AI, Python programming, machine learning, deep learning, and data analysis.

AI, Python and Agricultural Data

The session connected AI concepts with agricultural data and demonstrated how programming and analysis can support smarter, more data-driven farming decisions.

Machine Learning and Deep Learning

The lecture strengthened my understanding of how machine learning and deep neural networks can be connected to real agricultural challenges rather than treated only as abstract methods.

What I Learned

AI becomes meaningful when it is connected to a real problem. The value of an algorithm depends on the problem it helps understand or solve.

Day 3

Inside the Research Culture Through Laboratory Visits

Day 3 was one of the most important academic parts of the exchange. Instead of only learning in a classroom, I visited multiple laboratories and saw how researchers present their work, use equipment, discuss constraints, and connect theory with practice. Each image below represents a different laboratory or technical session.

Laboratory Visit

AI for Agriculture Research Lab

In this session, I explored how artificial intelligence is being applied to agricultural problems and how data-driven approaches can support smarter and more efficient farming practices.

What I Learned

Research starts by understanding the real problem clearly, not by beginning with an algorithm alone.

Laboratory Visit

Underwater Communication Laboratory

This lab introduced me to a communication problem where the physical environment itself changes what is technically possible. The experience connected strongly with my Information and Communication Engineering background.

What I Learned

Engineering solutions must always respond to the conditions of the environment in which they operate.

Laboratory Visit

Agricultural Robotics & Automated Grape Cultivation Lab

This was especially meaningful to me because it connected directly with my own interest in AI for agriculture. I could see how robotics, sensing, and automation move agricultural intelligence beyond image classification into an integrated physical system.

What I Learned

Real smart farming emerges when perception, sensing, robotics, and automation work together as one system.

Laboratory Visit

Communication Lab Presentation

Listening to project explanations inside the laboratory helped me see how researchers communicate the purpose, limitations, and technical constraints of a system while standing next to the real experimental setup.

What I Learned

Good research is also good communication — a strong system must be explained clearly and honestly.

Technical Session

3D Animation Project Session

The 3D and animation session introduced another way of constructing and communicating digital environments outside the core research tools I normally use.

What I Learned

Technical ideas can be explored and communicated through multiple forms of digital representation.

Day 3 changed the way I looked at a research laboratory — from a room full of equipment into a place where ideas, people, experiments, communication, and problem solving continuously interact.
Day 4

From Learning to Creating: The Ideathon

Day 4 challenged me to transform the knowledge from the previous sessions into a research idea of my own. I moved from learning existing technologies to identifying a practical agricultural problem and proposing an AI-driven solution.

Developing the Research Idea

During the Ideathon, I explored how AI could address a practical agricultural challenge in Bangladesh and worked through the process of shaping the problem into a structured research concept.

What I Learned

Research begins when learning turns into questioning, problem identification, and solution design.

Presenting My AI-for-Agriculture Concept

I presented “AI-Powered Rice Nutrient Deficiency Detection and Fertilizer-Pesticide Recommendation System”, focusing on using AI to identify rice nutrient deficiencies and support smarter agricultural decisions.

What I Learned

The most valuable ideas are the ones that continue growing after the presentation ends.

From Exchange Idea to Continuing Research

The concept did not remain only an Ideathon presentation. I later developed its nutrient-detection component further through Vision Transformer-based work on identifying healthy rice leaves and nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium deficiencies.

For me, Day 4 marked a transition from learning existing technologies to thinking more like a researcher — identifying a problem, proposing a solution, and communicating the idea in an international academic environment.
Day 5

Experiencing Japan Beyond the Laboratory

After several academically intensive days, Day 5 brought a cultural dimension to the exchange through a visit to Mount Fuji — one of the most memorable personal experiences of the journey.

Sakura Science Group at Mount Fuji

The group photograph captured a memorable cultural moment in the exchange, with Mount Fuji creating a remarkable backdrop to the experience.

A Personal Moment at Mount Fuji

Standing in front of Mount Fuji gave me a moment to step outside the academic schedule and connect more personally with Japan's landscape and cultural identity.

What I Learned

International education is also cultural education. Understanding the place and culture around an academic exchange adds depth to the relationships and memories built there.

Day 6

Tokyo Through Science, History and Culture

Day 6 brought together science and cultural exploration through visits to the Science Museum, the Sensō-ji Temple area in Asakusa, and the Tokyo National Museum.

Science Museum

I explored science and technology outside the formal university setting, seeing how scientific ideas can be presented through direct and accessible experiences.

What I Learned

Complex scientific ideas become more accessible when they are communicated through experience and interaction.

Kaminarimon Gate — Sensō-ji, Asakusa

Visiting the iconic Kaminarimon Gate introduced me to one of Tokyo's most recognizable historical and cultural settings.

What I Learned

Technological progress and cultural preservation can exist side by side within the same society.

Sensō-ji Temple Area

Walking through the temple area helped me experience a historical side of Tokyo and see how cultural heritage remains integrated into modern urban life.

Tokyo National Museum

The museum offered another perspective on Japan through its art, history, and preserved cultural heritage beyond the country's technological achievements.

What I Learned

Understanding innovation becomes richer when I also understand the history and culture from which it emerges.

Day 7

Farewell to Japan and Return Home

Day 7 marked the end of the physical journey, but not the end of its academic impact. I left Japan after a week of learning, laboratory exposure, research idea development, presentation, and cultural exploration.

A Final Group Moment at the Airport

Before departure, I joined the final group photograph with the Sakura Science team. It captured the closing moment of an intensive seven-day journey and the beginning of a new phase — applying the lessons and motivation from Japan to my own research path.

What I Took Home

I returned with a broader research perspective, stronger motivation, and a clearer interest in connecting artificial intelligence with meaningful real-world problems.

Final Reflection

What Sakura Changed for Me

The strongest value of the exchange was not any single lecture, laboratory, or photograph. It was the way those experiences collectively changed how I think about research and the kinds of problems I want to work on.

From Student to Researcher

I became more conscious of the difference between learning existing knowledge and shaping a research question of my own.

From AI Model to Real System

I learned to think beyond isolated algorithms and consider sensing, communication, robotics, automation, deployment, and practical constraints.

Academic Communication

I gained a clearer view of how researchers explain objectives, methods, limitations, and ideas within an experimental environment.

Global Research Perspective

The experience showed me how international collaboration can connect different academic cultures around shared technical problems.

A Lasting Academic Impact

I returned from Japan with stronger research motivation, greater interdisciplinary awareness, and a clearer ambition to connect artificial intelligence with real-world challenges — especially in agriculture and applied innovation.
